# Fresh-trade opening-range breakout (ORB)

`orb_trade_cross` is a separate, opt-in long trigger in **Config → Setups**. Choose the 5- or 15-minute option and save a custom setup in OR mode. It does not change `orb_breakout` or `orb_breakdown`: those remain completed-bar close-cross signals (`eventSemantics: "bar-close-cross"`). A trade-cross setup reports `eventSemantics: "trade-cross"` in `GET /api/v2/setups`, on its emitted alert and in `trigger_evidence`. Changing trigger or interval changes the saved and effective detector revisions. No existing setup is automatically converted or enabled.

The selected feed must support paired raw trade updates. The catalog's `tradeCrossCapability: "configured"` means the scanner can listen for them, **not** that any symbol is ready to enter. It is `"unavailable"` for disabled setups, detached evaluators and feeds without this capability. `GET /api/v2/setups/{setup_id}/check?symbol=AAPL` reports `tradeCrossStatus` by interval: `unavailable` (with reason), `continuity_unavailable`, `armed`, or `fired_today`. A consumer must wait for an actual live alert and still apply its own market, spread, account and risk checks.

## Source and crossing rule

The existing single Schwab stream subscribes to both CHART_EQUITY and LEVELONE_EQUITIES. [Schwabdev's stream field map](https://github.com/tylerebowers/schwabdev/blob/main/docs/pages-raw/stream.md) identifies Level One field `3` as last trade price and `35` as trade time; Level One messages are sparse changes. Edge only treats an update as a candidate trade when **both raw fields occur in that same update** and the quote book accepts the matching price/time. A carried-forward `last`, a field-34 quote clock, a WebSocket receipt time, a REST poll, or a minute-bar high cannot create a trade crossing. This is the first *observed* qualifying Level One update, not a claim to see every exchange print.

The opening high comes from the exact completed 09:30–09:34 ET or 09:30–09:44 ET one-minute bars. Every minute must exist and come from Schwab CHART_EQUITY or Schwab's historical one-minute endpoint; quote-synthesized bars cannot establish this range. `trade_cross.opening_range` carries its interval, high/low, start/end and an ID that includes a digest of the constituent bars. A corrected opening bar changes that ID and clears armed continuity. The trade must occur at or after 09:35 or 09:45 ET respectively and before 16:00 ET.

After the range is complete, Edge needs a fresh, same-stream observation **at or below** the high, followed by a strictly higher trade price with a later trade market timestamp. The two observations must be no more than 10 seconds apart. The crossing update must arrive within 2 seconds of its own market time, not be delayed, and have live stream coverage and valid bid/ask quality. Duplicates, older times, stale updates, disconnects/reconnects, gaps and range corrections cannot manufacture continuity. A startup price already above the high does not fire; status stays `continuity_unavailable` until a below-side observation arrives. A valid crossing consumes this symbol/interval's once-per-Eastern-day opportunity, even if a downstream universe filter blocks publication. On normal restart, archived same-day trade-cross alerts restore the latch; an archive write failure or missing archive prevents a cross-process guarantee. This contract does not rearm on a recross that day.

The alert's `market_timestamp` and `timestamp` are the crossing trade time; `price` is that trade price. `trade_cross` also carries the local receipt timestamp and its market-to-receipt `latency_ms`, plus stream ID, connection epoch, coverage and quote quality. `source_bar` remains the latest completed one-minute bar used as setup/gate context, **not** the crossing trade or a forming candle. `trigger_evidence[].trade_cross` preserves the same trade/range evidence. The quote snapshot, when present, is separate from the trade evidence. Trader must reject missing provenance, unsupported/unknown semantics, replay/legacy mode, stale evidence, or an unavailable continuity status; Edge never authorizes an order.

## Latency and limitations

An on-host read-only sample on 2026-09-23 at approximately 12:57 PT inspected the last 1,000 Level One quote-book updates. Of 426 stream observations where a symbol's **trade market clock advanced** and the market-to-receipt difference was 0–10 seconds, the median was **354 ms**, the 95th percentile **867 ms**, and the maximum **1,099 ms**. This measures the observed feed leg only; it is not a paired-field audit or a guarantee of end-to-end alert latency. The deterministic crossing fixture uses 300 ms market-to-receipt delay and verifies publication before the next completed minute bar. Each actual trade-cross alert exposes its own `latency_ms` and `emitted_at` so consumers can measure market-to-publication delay and set a stricter rejection threshold.

Level One is change-based rather than a complete time-and-sales feed. Separate price/time fields, dropped updates, skipped intermediate trades, an unverified opening minute, or a quote tier that is polled rather than streamed can make this capability unavailable; Edge does **not** upgrade them to `trade-cross`. Existing bar-close ORB remains available for chart screening under its different timing semantics.
